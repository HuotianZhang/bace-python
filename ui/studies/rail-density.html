The bench rail, at four densities

Below 1560 the rail folds into two rows of four, and on a 1440 window it takes — — a whole block at the top of the screen, gone before any measured result appears. Below are the current style and three ways of compressing it, over the same real readings and the same DOM: every difference is in the CSS. Not one line of rail.js moves, and ui/tests/rail.test.mjs is unaffected. The heights are measured after the page loads, not estimated.

Outcome: C is merged into ui/style.css. It measures 27 px in the real console (the sample on this page is 29 px; the difference is the page shell's font fallback), stays on one line at 1280, 1440 and 1920 alike, and truncates neither labels nor values. The relay cell is 248 px with a 96 px diagram; the sub-line became a panel that drops on hover or Tab focus, and lib/rail.js gives cells that have one a tabindex and a title. This page stays as the record of what A and B each saved, and what C traded away for its own.

ui-rules section 1: “density is a requirement, not a preference… if a screen feels roomy, it is probably hiding something the operator needs.” A and B hide nothing; C hides seven of the eight sub-lines — among them the LED's (pulse · POL INV ✓ · low 0.400 V), which ui-rules section 10 names as one that must stay visible: the low level has to sit below the turn-on voltage for the dark half-cycle to really be dark. Whether C is worth that is a judgement call; this page only sets out the account.
