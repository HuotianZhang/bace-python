The bench page head, four layouts

On a 1440 window with the identity panel collapsed, the first parameter appears at — (397 px in the real console; the difference is this page shell's font fallback), or 540 px with the identity panel open, which is half the screen. A PR last week compressed the bench rail from 156 px to 27 px, and this week a 65 px Bench settings block arrived on the bench page — part of what was saved has been handed straight back.

Two faults, different in kind. One: a sixfold difference in density — the bench rail fits eight readings into one 27 px row, while the Instruments panel right beside it fits three switches into three rows and 172 px, 57 px a row, the loosest block in the whole console. Two: nothing states the scope of the Save — three save semantics are stacked down the screen: a switch acts on the bench the moment it is clicked, a card field PUTs as soon as it changes, and Bench settings has an explicit Save — and that Save sits between the switches and the cards, belonging to neither.

The second is the root. The fix is not to make it shorter but to put it back inside the boundary of what it saves: it saves the cards below, so it belongs on the MODULES row — a row that was already there (26 px), an empty ::before pseudo-element at the time. And the fourth question of ui-rules section 14, how often is it touched, decides the rest: something used less than once a day should not live on screen, so it goes behind a .... Only one thing stays resident — which saved bench this is, and whether it has moved since.

Outcome: B is merged. It measures 185 px in the real console (the sample on this page is 196; the difference is the page shell's font fallback), Instruments is one 42 px row, and 1280 / 1440 / 1920 neither fold nor truncate. That ... sits immediately after the identity, not at the end of the row — the first version pushed it to the end of a 1440-wide row, 1300 px from the MODULES label it belongs to, and nobody could find it. ui-rules section 14 now records that.

Where save / load actually lives: the three states of the MODULES row

Only one row stays on screen, and it answers one question: which saved bench is this, and has it moved since? The machinery -- naming, Save, the list of saved benches, the difference table -- lives behind the ... at the end of the row: something used less than once a day should not occupy the screen (the fourth question of ui-rules section 14). A scope does the same thing: Save/Recall sits behind a front-panel key and a menu, and never takes up the measurement display.

Once the ... is opened

Save and load lose nothing -- the name field, Save settings, the list of saved benches, and opening one draws the difference table, with the button below that table being the one that actually moves the bench. Only two things change: it is no longer resident, and it now grows below the MODULES row — so its scope is self-evident. It saves the cards below it, not the row of switches above.

This is not the power panel's .... That one is a <details> that closes itself the moment the pointer leaves, which is right for options that are done in one click; here there is typing to do and a difference table to read, and something that vanishes when the mouse slides off is unusable. So this drawer stays open once opened, until the ... is clicked again or the save or load is finished.

All three satisfy the fourth question of ui-rules section 14, that a control touched less than once a day should not be on screen; only B and C answer section 1, that density is a requirement, seriously. And section 14 also says a switch throws a relay on one click and so needs a larger target — C makes that target the smallest of the three and hides the LED's low level behind hover, which is exactly the account C was charged with in rail-density.html (the low level has to sit below the turn-on voltage for the dark half-cycle to really be dark). B is the one chosen: 209 px saved, not one reading hidden, and it fits at 1280. C saves only 16 px more than B and wants the LED's four levels and the switches' targets in exchange — a bad trade. This page stays to keep that account.
