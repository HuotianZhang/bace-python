Measurement structure

Anatomy of a BACE Run

What the experiment is, before any question of how to code it — the atomic shot, the axes that can be swept over it, and where the numbers turn into physics.

1 · The shot

The illumination is not continuous. A 33220A switches the LED between a drive level that lights it and one below its turn-on threshold — a 1 kHz square, 50 % duty. The LED is fully on or fully off; there is no dimming, so the on-phase intensity is exactly the intensity used for the DC measurement.

An 81150A holds the device at Vpre ≈ Voc through the 500 µs on-phase. Near Voc almost no current flows, so carriers accumulate and the device settles at its light open-circuit condition. A fixed delay after the LED goes off, the 81150A steps to a reverse collection field Vcoll and sweeps them out into the sense resistor.

The reference run repeats the identical experiment in the dark, over the same bias swing: the whole window is translated down by Voc, from 0 → Vcoll − Voc instead of Voc → Vcoll. Same ΔV, so it measures the charge that is not photogenerated — the capacitive charge displaced by that swing, plus whatever dark current flows. Translating rather than repeating the absolute range is what keeps the diode out of forward bias, where in the dark it would inject heavily at 0.8 V.

The invariant that makes Voc the right axis centre

One number is used twice within an intensity point, and everything downstream assumes they match:

V oc measurement   FUNC:SHAP DC;    :VOLT:OFFS  1.0          →  V oc = 0.8 V
transient          FUNC:SHAP PULSE; :VOLT:HIGH  1.0
                                    :VOLT:LOW   0.4   (dark)
                                    :FREQ 1000;  FUNC:PULS:DCYC 50

Because the LED is fully off below ~1 V rather than dimmed, there is no duty-cycle averaging: the on-phase intensity is the DC intensity, and the Voc measured under DC is the condition the prebias sits at. If the pulse high level ever drifts from the DC level the axis is centred on the wrong Voc, every charge is biased, and nothing in the data shows it. That is worth an assertion rather than a convention — core/illumination.py refuses a low level above threshold, and assert_axis_centre refuses a Voc measured at a different drive.

How the two generators are told apart in the recovered code

The SCPI written inline by BACE_Mehrdad.vi carries no channel index — FUNC:SHAP PULSE;, :FREQ %g;:VOLT:HIGH %g;:VOLT:LOW %g;, FUNC:PULS:DCYC %g; — while every 81150A command in its driver library is indexed: :FUNC%d, :VOLT%d:HIGH, :PULS:DEL%d, :ARM:SOUR%d. So the inline block is the 33220A, set up as a pulse. The 81150A is armed from it and carries the delay.

The per-step acquisition in the engine confirms the light/dark pair: two Agilent 81150StandardWaveform calls and two shutter_lv2012 calls per point, waveform and shutter changed together between them.

2 · The structure

A run is that shot, repeated along an axis. Which axis you sweep is the choice of experiment — the hardware, the waveform and the arithmetic are identical across all of them.

The one thing that stops it being a plain nested loop over a fixed grid: the prebias axis is centred on a quantity the rig has to measure first. Voc moves with illumination, so the axis has to be built after the SourceMeter has spoken, once per intensity.

This generalisation is already latent in your LabVIEW

The engine computes Vcoll1Loop and Delay1Loop as per-step arrays, one entry for every point on the sweep — and then fills both with a constant (Vpre1Loop*0 + Vcoll). The machinery for a delay or collection-field axis is there and has never been given a value. Making the axis a parameter rather than a hard-coded choice is not a new feature; it is finishing one.

3 · What the charge becomes

One systematic the subtraction cannot remove

The two runs share a step amplitude, not a step range: the light run traverses +0.8 → −2.5 V, the dark run 0 → −3.3 V. The displaced charge is ∫C(V) dV over each range, so the two cancel exactly only where C is flat across the swing. Junction capacitance is not flat near forward bias, so a residual survives — small when Vcoll is deep and most of the swing is in reverse, larger for shallow Vcoll or thin devices. It is not a reason to change the method; it is a reason to keep both raw traces, so the assumption stays checkable after the sample is gone.

Q on its own is not the answer. Two corrections and one derivative stand between the raw integral and a carrier density, and both corrections are the reason the sweep exists in the first place.

Three things this structure fixes

  • The axis becomes data. BACE and TDCF stop being two programs sharing an engine by copy-paste and become two axis definitions. A Vcoll series — field dependence of extraction — comes free, having never been available before.
  • The Voc dependency becomes visible. Right now it is implicit in which frame of the case structure runs first. Drawn as an arrow, it is obvious that a stale or failed Voc reading silently poisons the whole axis beneath it — worth an explicit check rather than a wrong sweep centre.
  • One place where traces are kept, not two. The engine already saves them — per-loop and averaged light, dark and photocurrent, eight files zipped per measurement. The wrapper does not: its summary file holds only Q. So the traces exist for a standalone run and vanish for an automated series. One storage layer subscribing to one event stream fixes that, and makes every processing decision — baseline window, integration start, offset correction — revisitable after the sample is gone.
