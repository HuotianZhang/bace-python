2026-09-02 correction

The conclusion of the section “the light still does not go out” does not hold. Measured with a Newport 1918-C: at the 0.4 V low level the LED puts out 0.009 % of the conducting level — it is dark. What was seen instead, an extraction current that does not decay within 4 µs, has another cause: the device rests at V_oc under the wrong polarity combination.

The section “the baseline correction subtracted the signal away” does hold, and has reached the code: core.process.baseline_is_flat() now warns when the head and tail of a record disagree (changing no number). trigger_offset_s has also gone from 47 ns to the measured 0.

one · the proof

INV rests the device at V_oc

The raw light − dark of both runs (no baseline correction), at the same delay = +80 ns. The NORM trace extracts continuously before the pulse and the pulse switches extraction off; the INV trace is zero before the pulse and the pulse switches extraction on. The latter is BACE.

two · the trap

The baseline correction subtracted the signal away

Two versions of the same INV curve: solid is the raw difference, dashed is the photo actually stored in the HDF5. photocurrent(offset_correct=True) subtracts the mean of the last 10 % of the record — and here the last 10 % is not baseline but the steady-state extraction current itself.

how serious this is

The baseline correction is not wrong in itself, and the archived LabVIEW does the same thing: once a transient has decayed to zero, the tail really is baseline. It fails only when the tail is still signal — which is exactly the symptom of the light not going out. Two faults stacked together produce a wrong answer with nothing anomalous about it.

core.process.baseline_is_flat() has been added: it compares the head and the tail of the record, measured against the full span. It changes no number and only raises a warning at run time — the tail is not baseline, offset_correct is subtracting signal, Q will look reasonable and be wrong, and both raw traces are stored so it can be repaired offline. Three tests hold it down, one of them using this round’s own numbers.

three · still does not hold

The light still does not go out

The extraction current after the pulse does not decay at all within 4 µs: 1.80 → 1.91 mA, then flat to the end of the record. In BACE this curve should return to zero once the carriers have been swept out. It does not — the device is being driven from V_oc to −1 V under steady illumination, so what is measured is a steady photocurrent, not a transient photocharge.

This agrees with the first round’s conclusion (which came from finding no light-off step in the record, with an upper bound of 1.3 %); this round simply sees it from another direction: no decay means the source never went off.

Confirmed along the way: bias_output_polarity = INV, bias_polarity_source = set by this run, led_output_polarity = INV, bias_arm_source = EXT and bias_arm_slope = POS are all written into the HDF5’s /config/resolved (schema bace-run/2). The gap the previous round exposed — a file that cannot say which convention it was measured under — is verified on real hardware.
