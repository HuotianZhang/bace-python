2026-09-02 correction

Section two’s suspicion that the light does not go off is wrong. The LED was later measured directly with a Newport 1918-C: at the 0.4 V low level it puts out 0.009 % of the conducting level, so the light really does go out. Whatever kept the light-off step out of the record, it was not the LED.

Also, trigger_offset_s was still 47 ns when this page was written; measured, it is 0. The criterion for whether a curve is usable has narrowed too: light minus dark must return to zero in the steady state. The full conclusions are in bace-wrapup.html.

three · what it reads

Three things hold, two do not

holds
  • No clipping. Light and dark do not share an extreme, and the extreme repeats once. The first batch of fixes works on real data.
  • The response follows :PULS:DEL1 exactly. Taking each curve’s 50 % crossing of the step and fitting a line against delay: slope 1.0003, residual rms 1.46 ns, intercept 594.1 ns. Whatever is written is exactly how much later it arrives at the device — there is no hidden scaling in the chain pulse_levels() → instrument → device.
  • The whole tool chain ran. Preflight → 147 shots → a complete data set (HDF5 plus five .dat files). The waveforms were kept, so the integration window could be recomputed offline — which is what saved this analysis.
does not hold

One: the light going out is not in the record. Take the points with delay ≥ 120 ns (the pulse step is past 714 ns, so 622 ns is clean) and average 540–690 ns: the level changes by −1.4e−7 A across the window, peak to peak 2.5e−5 A, against a plateau of 1.93e−3 A in the same batch. Any light-off step must be smaller than 1.3 % of the plateau — where switching the light off should drop the photocurrent by 100 %. The light is on throughout the record.

Two: there is therefore no knee to find. The delay axis is defined as the time between the light going out and extraction; if the light never goes out, the axis sweeps a delay relative to an event that does not happen. The slow decline in Q(delay) (the plateau level before the step drifted only −14 % as well) is something else, not recombination decay. This is not a matter of insufficient signal to noise.

four · why

Before the pulse it is +2 mA, not zero

That number says qualitatively where the device sits on each side of the pulse:

before the pulse   photo = +2.0 mA   reverse bias, extracting photocarriers continuously
after the pulse    photo ~ 0          raised to near V_oc, net current zero

In other words, the pulse is the step into prebias, not the extraction: the baseline level is the extracting one. The extraction edge is at :PULS:DEL1 + 5 µs, outside the record window. This is exactly the case the comments in run-delay.toml describe.

Which bit needs flipping — the instrument’s :OUTP1:POL or the device’s own terminals — this file cannot tell: the waveforms are identical either way. This round used output_polarity = "leave" (the wiring was uncertain, so nothing was touched), and a guess should not be filled in here. The reward for not touching it is that the waveform itself says “this ran backwards” with no assumption about the wiring; the cost is that which way to flip has to come from the next experiment, not this one.

As for why the light is on throughout: two candidates, both testable. (a) the arm-trigger phase in this round differs from the one during --sync; (b) the LED’s “off” level of 0.4 V still emits — in which case the light never actually goes out, and photo (against a shutter-closed dark) would be a constant, which is exactly what is seen.

five · next

Separate (a) from (b) before talking about the zero

  • Run --sync again and the scan immediately after, touching nothing in between. It reports where the arm trigger sits relative to the light. If it still says armed on light-off, (a) is ruled out and the problem is the LED’s off level.
  • Test whether the LED’s “off” really is off: drop the 33220A low level from 0.4 V to 0 and see whether photo’s steady value moves. Unchanged means 0.4 V never emitted; changed means it has been emitting all along, and the 1.0 V threshold in illumination.py does not hold for this LED.
  • Polarity: do not guess, lengthen the record. Drop pulse_width_ns to 2000 and both edges fall inside the 5 µs record — which edge is the extraction is then visible at a glance, with no need to decide NORM or INV first.
  • SNR. Three loops scatter too much point to point. Loops 3 → 20, averages 20 → 100, and vcoll to −4 V once clipping has been ruled out.

The scan range needs changing too. If the recombination lifetime is of order microseconds (the fit gives τ ~ 1.1 µs, degenerate with a straight line though it is), then 0–200 ns can only show a few percent of decay. The range should be microseconds — but then the pulse runs outside the 5 µs record, so the timebase and pulse_width have to move together.
