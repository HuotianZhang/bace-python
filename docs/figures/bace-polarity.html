2026-09-02 correction

Two claims on this page are withdrawn. “The device may be damaged” — it is not: the J–V gives V_oc 1.02771 V, J_sc −126.127 A/m², FF 0.5505, a good pixel. “The light does not go off” — also untrue: measured with the power meter, the low level puts out 0.009 % of the conducting level.

The criterion was later narrowed to: light minus dark must return to zero in the steady state (not “where V_oc landed”). five · what the curves say about the sign

Two runs contradict each other

The account given is that the Keithley is reversed and the amplifier is normal, so the device electrodes are the wrong way round. The curves hold two independent clues, and they point opposite ways:

For “the device is the right way round” (the amplifier’s positive = the code’s assumed positive): the 17:59 run had its baseline at +0.92 V of amplifier output and measured light − dark = +0.04 mA ≈ 0. And 0.92 is exactly the |V_oc| the Keithley read at the time (0.9187 / 0.9222). The same run, stepping to −1.00 V, gave 1.91 mA. Were the device reversed, +0.92 V would be reverse bias at full extraction and could not be zero. This clue uses magnitudes only, and is independent of the current’s sign convention.

For “the device is reversed”: the 18:15 dark baseline sat at +2.80 V of amplifier output and measured only 0.13 mA. Were the device the right way round that is +2.80 V of forward bias — and on the J–V, +1.5 V already gives 2034 A/m² (~16 × Jsc), so the current should be tens of mA. Reversed, it is −2.80 V of reverse bias, and a small current makes sense. The same arithmetic on 18:05 comes out the other way: that run had a dark baseline of −1.92 V and measured −2.6 mA, which the right way round (reverse-bias leakage) explains and reversed (+1.92 V forward) does not.

So these files cannot settle the sign; the two runs fight each other. One way out: if the amplifier does not actually reach its level at some settings (current limiting), then “the current is small” is not reliable evidence. The cleanest answer is still to run ③, or to put a probe directly on the amplifier output, give the 81150A a known positive level and see whether it is positive or negative there — one number, and the whole ambiguity ends.

Also: the earlier claim that the 18:15 combination “pushes the device to +2.80 V of forward bias in the dark, with a risk of damage” — if the device really is reversed then that is −2.80 V of reverse bias and there is no such risk. That warning is withdrawn until the sign is settled. The J–V also shows the device is fine.
