Not the difference but the two curves themselves. The displacement-current spike very nearly coincides between the light and dark branches — as it should. What actually distinguishes the four combinations is the direction of that spike, and where each curve comes to rest before and after the step.

The y axis follows each panel’s own extreme, so the spike is legible. ①’s spike points up (+20 mA) and the other three point down — which is exactly a change of sign. ① differs by 1.85 mA between light and dark before the step and both go to zero after it; ② is the reverse, differing by 1.9 / 4.5 mA after the step and never decaying; ④ differs by 0.047 mA before the step and reads light 0.010, dark 0.055 mA after — both curves return to zero, so of course the difference does too. It is the only one of the four with nothing left over once extraction has finished.

Note too that ④’s light and dark minima are exactly equal (−20.27 mA). That is neither coincidence nor physics: two independently averaged acquisitions do not agree to the float, and both are pressed against the same range floor. So ④’s peak height is unusable — but only 11 ns is clipped away, and the charge integrals over the seven delay points still scatter by only 4%.
