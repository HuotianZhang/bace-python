# Validity

The conditions under which Q is the complete excess charge, and n = |Q|/(eAd) a bulk density, and the measurements that test them. Each test is a run on the device itself; none is made automatically.

## Completeness

Q is complete when every carrier stored at V_oc reaches a contact inside the window.

| Test | Run | Q is complete when |
|---|---|---|
| Collection voltage | Axis `vcoll`, V_coll from about −1 V to the most negative value the device tolerates | \|Q\| no longer grows with \|V_coll\|. Growth means recombination or space charge during the extraction (2.1). |
| Delay | Axis `delay_ns`, at least one value below and several above t_d ≈ 75 ns | Q falls smoothly with t_d once the light is out. Below the light-off time the field extracts while carriers are still generated, so Q(t_d) has a knee there: it locates the light going out at the sample (2.2). The decay above the knee is recombination before the extraction. Extrapolated to the light-off time, it gives the charge at the moment the light went out. |
| Window | `tools/reintegrate.py` over the stored traces, or a run with a longer record | \|Q\| against the window width levels off before the chosen width (2.2). |
| Null | `light_shutter = "shut"` and `dark_reference = "same"` | Q is zero within its error; the late level of light − dark is the drift of 2.3. |

At the delay used for a measurement, recombination during the interval t_d − 75 ns has already removed some charge. The delay scan states how much.

## Bulk density

n = |Q|/(eAd), with A the device area and d the active-layer thickness, is the extracted excess carrier density. It approximates the mean density of either carrier when the electron and hole densities are equal and their profiles nearly uniform, and it is the bulk density only when the bulk holds most of the charge. Near each contact a space-charge region holds charge that does not depend on recombination in the bulk:

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Thick layer, at least about 200 nm | The contact regions are a small part of the layer |
| High intensity | The bulk density exceeds the charge in the contact regions |
| Injection barriers at the contacts | Less charge is injected into the contact regions in the dark |
| Thin layer, ohmic contacts, low intensity | The contact regions dominate: n no longer follows the bulk, and the recombination order from n against G (2.1) comes out above 2 |

Source: Kniepert et al., *J. Appl. Phys.* (2019), doi:10.1063/1.5129037, from drift-diffusion simulation.

Compare n against G at several LED levels. An apparent order above 2, or a density that changes little with intensity, can indicate that the contact regions dominate; it does not establish the cause, since density-dependent recombination and energetic disorder have similar effects. Devices of several thicknesses separate contact and surface effects from the bulk. The dark charge at V_pre = V_oc (`light_shutter = "shut"`, 2.1), compared with Q, shows how much the light changes the carrier profiles at the same voltage; it does not divide Q into parts.

## Absolute calibration

A capacitor of known capacitance C_ref in place of the device tests the whole chain from R_s to the integral. A step ΔV moves the charge

    Q_ref = C_ref · ΔV

through the sense resistor. The dark subtraction removes this charge by design, so Q_ref is the integral of a single trace: the light trace stored in the run file (3.3), with the baseline before the trigger and the run's window. Its magnitude divided by C_ref·\|ΔV\| should be 1; a different ratio is the combined error of R_s, the digitiser's vertical scale and the window. Its sign follows `current_sign` and is checked separately.

| Choice | Guidance |
|---|---|
| C_ref | Close to the device's geometric capacitance, so the current and the vertical range are those of a measurement; a low-loss film or ceramic type |
| ΔV | Two or more values of V_pre − V_coll; Q_ref must scale with ΔV |
| Window | As in a measurement; a capacitor's current decays within the spike, so the width does not matter |

A capacitor whose C does not depend on V must also give Q = 0 with the translated dark reference: this tests the subtraction. The device's own C(V) term (2.1) needs a C(V) measurement of the device over both voltage ranges.
