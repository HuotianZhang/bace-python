# Charge extraction

What the extracted charge is, in terms of the carrier densities in the device. The derivation follows Kniepert's thesis, ch. 5, and the papers listed in `../bace-principle.md`.

## Steady state at open circuit

Under steady illumination at V_oc no current leaves the device. Integrated over the active layer, the generation rate therefore equals the recombination rate:

    G d = ∫ R(x) dx

with G the generation rate per volume, d the active-layer thickness and R(x) the local recombination rate. Surface recombination at the contacts adds to the right-hand side.

V_oc measures the quasi-Fermi level splitting at the contacts. In the bulk, where the splitting is uniform and equal to eV_oc, the electron and hole densities satisfy

    n p = n_i² exp(eV_oc / kT)

For equal electron and hole densities in a nondegenerate bulk, n = p = n_i exp(eV_oc / 2kT): the density at V_oc is set by V_oc. Trapped charge and doping break the equality and are treated separately. Raising the light raises V_oc and with it n. With R = k_δ n^δ, a series of LED levels gives n against G, and

    δ = d ln G / d ln n

is the recombination order. G is not measured directly: the usual estimate is G = J_sc/(e d), which assumes that every carrier is collected at short circuit.

## Extraction under reverse bias

The bias steps from V_pre to the reverse voltage V_coll as the light goes out. The step strengthens the field in the layer, which drives electrons and holes to opposite contacts. Neglecting space charge, the field after the step is about (V_bi − V_coll)/d, with V_bi the built-in voltage. Two processes compete with extraction:

| Process | Effect on Q | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Recombination during the extraction | Carriers lost before they reach a contact; Q too small | A larger \|V_coll\| shortens the transit time, d²/(μ(V_bi − V_coll)), with μ the slower carrier's mobility. |
| Space charge | A stored charge comparable to C_geo·\|V_bi − V_coll\| screens the applied field, and extraction slows | A larger \|V_coll\|, or a lower LED level |

A plateau of \|Q\| against \|V_coll\| shows that extraction outruns these losses at the chosen delay and window; the delay and window tests of 2.4 complete the check.

The extraction also carries the charge on the electrodes. The geometric capacitance C_geo of the layer takes C_geo·(V_pre − V_coll) when the bias steps. This charge does not come from the carriers in the layer, and the dark reference removes it.

## The dark reference

The dark trace repeats the same voltage step without light. With `dark_reference = "translated"` it starts at 0 V instead of V_pre and keeps the swing V_pre − V_coll. Four states are involved:

| Trace | Before the step | After the step |
|---|---|---|
| light | n(G, V_oc) | n(0, V_coll) |
| dark | n(0, 0) | n(0, V_coll − V_oc) |

Here n(G, V) is the carrier density at generation rate G and bias V; G = 0 is the dark. The excess carrier density is the light trace's change minus the dark trace's:

    n_exc = [n(G, V_oc) − n(0, V_coll)] − [n(0, 0) − n(0, V_coll − V_oc)]
          ≈ n(G, V_oc) − n(0, 0)

since at large reverse bias the device is nearly empty in both final states, n(0, V_coll) ≈ n(0, V_coll − V_oc).

| Term | Fate in the difference |
|---|---|
| Electrode charge C_geo·ΔV | Cancels: both steps have the same swing ΔV. A residual remains where the integrals of the device's C(V) over the two voltage ranges differ. |
| Carriers stored at V_oc | Remain: this is Q. |
| Carriers in the dark at 0 V | Subtracted: n(0, 0), small in a device without doping. |
| Dark current during the extraction | Nearly cancels: both traces end at large reverse bias. Leakage differs by the change of reverse current between V_coll and V_coll − V_oc. |

`dark_reference = "same"` repeats the light trace's exact levels in the dark. It cancels C_geo·ΔV exactly but subtracts n(0, V_oc), which at V_oc is nearly the whole signal (next section). It is a control and a null measurement, not a measurement of n.

## Light and bias at V_oc

At V_oc the light and an applied voltage of V_oc set the same quasi-Fermi level splitting, and so nearly the same density. In an ideal device n(G, V_oc) and n(0, V_oc) are equal; the light sets V_oc, and V_oc sets n.

The density at V_oc is therefore not the sum of a photogenerated part and an injected part. A trace taken in the dark at V_pre = V_oc (`light_shutter = "shut"`) measures nearly the same carriers as the light trace, not a separate contribution. Their ratio says how far the device is from the ideal case: contact space-charge regions, traps and inhomogeneous profiles make the two densities differ.

The dark charge at forward bias has its own use: compared with a drift-diffusion simulation, it estimates the injection barrier at the contacts.
