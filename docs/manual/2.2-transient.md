# The transient

The current that follows the voltage step, term by term, and the timing that places the integration window on it.

## Displacement current

The step charges the device's capacitance through the series resistance of the circuit: the amplifier's output, the cable and R_s. For a step of height ΔV, a capacitance C and a series resistance R:

    I(t) = (ΔV / R) exp(−t / RC),    ∫ I dt = C ΔV

The amplifier's rise time rounds the leading edge. The resulting spike is the largest feature of both traces and nearly the same in each:

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Spike edge | 6 ns on a good shot | `analysis/2026-09-06/spike-lag-and-dark-charge.md` |
| Spike 1/e decay, dark reference from 0 V | 19–21 ns, 220–290 K | same |
| Spike 1/e decay, from V_oc without light | 20–29.5 ns, rising with temperature | same |

The decay from 0 V does not change with temperature; the decay from V_oc does. The circuit is therefore not what changes: the decay from V_oc includes the carriers stored in the device, which leave with the displacement current. The light trace and the dark trace consequently differ in shape over the spike, and their difference there is signal, not a timing error.

The spike's charge cancels in light − dark except for the residual of C(V) over the two voltage ranges (2.1). A trigger that moves between the traces leaves the spike in the difference. The timing check (4.2) fails a shot when the spikes are more than 0.25 ns apart and an edge is slower than 8 ns, or when the Sync edges of the two traces are more than 0.1 ns apart.

## Extraction current

After the spike, light − dark is the current of the carriers stored at V_oc. It has two parts:

| Part | Time scale | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| Transit | t_tr ≈ d²/(μ(V_bi − V_coll)), tens to hundreds of nanoseconds | The mobility of the slower carrier and the field |
| Tail | Microseconds | Carriers released from traps, and the slowest carriers crossing a field screened by space charge |

The tail decides how long the integration window must be. It depends on the device and on the temperature, so it is measured on each device (2.4). On sample "test" at 294 K and V_coll −4 V, with the mean of a null run subtracted, the charge collected relative to a 15 µs window is 80 % at 1.5 µs, 96 % at 6 µs and 98 % at 8 µs (`analysis/2026-10-26/scope-window/`).

## Timing

The 81150A's Sync triggers the digitiser and is t = 0 of every record. Two paths leave the 33220A's edge that switches the LED off:

| Path | Event | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Light | 33220A drive edge | −380 ns |
| | LED driver, about 83 ns, and 85 m of fibre, 419 ns | |
| | Light off at the sample | +122 ns |
| Bias | 33220A Sync arms the 81150A; 81150A Sync, digitiser trigger | 0 |
| | 81150A output, after `:PULS:DEL1` = t_d | t_d |
| | Amplifier and cable: the latency `trigger_offset_s`, 47.1 ns | |
| | Field at the device | t_d + 47.1 ns |

Sources: `figures/bace-timing.html` (light path, measured with a Si detector in the device's place) and `rig.toml [electrical] trigger_offset_s` (fitted to the spike position over a delay scan, 1.5 ns residual). `:PULS:DEL1` delays the 81150A's output and not its Sync, so the transient moves through the record as t_d changes.

The interval from the light going out to the field arriving is

    t_d + 47.1 ns − 122 ns = t_d − 75 ns

It is 15 ns at t_d = 90 ns. Below t_d ≈ 75 ns the field arrives while the light is still on at the sample, and the measured charge includes carriers generated during the extraction. The light-off time is measured with a Si detector; the device's own response places the delay zero to about ±15 ns (`figures/bace-timing.html`), and a delay scan checks the alignment on the device itself (2.4).

Carriers recombine between the light going out and the field arriving. A delay scan, Q against t_d, measures that decay.

## Integration window

The window is defined relative to the field arrival, not to the trigger or the start of the record, so it moves with t_d and every point of a delay scan integrates the same part of its own transient. Measured from the first sample of the record, it starts at

    t_d + trigger_offset_s + t0_int_s − trace_t0

and lasts `t_int_width_s`, with `trace_t0` the record's origin relative to the trigger (−1 µs at 1000 ns/div). The run reports the window in record time with its first point, and stores it with every point.

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `t0_int_s` | −2 ns | Starts the window before the spike, so no extraction current is lost. Light − dark is zero before the field arrives, so an earlier start adds only noise. |
| `t_int_width_s` | 6 µs | Collects 96 % of the charge relative to a 15 µs window on the slowest device measured, while the noise from drift between the traces, which grows with the width (2.3), stays at about 0.012 nC per loop |
| `timebase_ns_per_div` | 1000 | A 10 µs record: 1 µs before the trigger for the baseline, 9 µs after it for the window |
| `pulse_width_ns` | 10000 | The bias returns to V_pre after the record ends, so the return step is not recorded |

A wider window recovers the last few per cent of a slow tail at the cost of noise:

| Width | Standard deviation of Q per loop | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5 µs | 0.004 nC | `analysis/2026-10-26/scope-window/` |
| 6 µs | 0.012 nC | same |
| 15 µs | 0.034 nC | same |

Choose the width by measuring Q against the window on the device (2.4). `tools/reintegrate.py` recomputes Q for other windows from a run's stored traces.
