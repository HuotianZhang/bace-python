# The bench

Six instruments, a shutter and a relay, controlled by one service on the lab PC. This chapter says what each part does in a shot and how the signals run between them. The values quoted are those in `rig.toml`, which is the authority for the hardware.

## Instruments

| Instrument | Address | Role in a shot | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agilent 33220A function generator | `GPIB0::15` | Drives the LED: 500 Hz, 50 % duty, high level `level_v`, low level below the LED threshold. Its Sync output arms the 81150A. | `bace/drivers/agilent33220a.py` |
| Agilent 81150A pulse generator | `GPIB0::12` | Generates the bias step V_pre → V_coll after `delay_ns`, through the ×4 bias amplifier. Its Trigger Out (the Sync) triggers the digitiser. | `bace/drivers/agilent81150.py` |
| Keysight Infiniium DSO9054H oscilloscope | LAN, `PwM-DSO9054H.local` | Records the current signal on CHAN2, the 81150A Sync on CHAN3 and, for a timing check, a photodiode at the sample on CHAN4; averages N_avg acquisitions. | `bace/drivers/infiniium.py` |
| Keithley 2400 source meter | `GPIB0::24` | Measures V_oc at zero current, J_sc and J–V curves. Not used during a shot. | `bace/drivers/keithley2400.py` |
| Lake Shore 331 temperature controller | `GPIB0::7` | Holds the cryostat setpoint; read every 5 s. | `bace/drivers/lakeshore331/` |
| Newport 1918-C power meter | USB | Reads the light power from a beam splitter during a run. | `bace/drivers/newport1918c/` |
| Deditec DIO, module ID 9 | USB | Module 0, channel 0 moves the shutter (1 = open); module 1, channel 0 moves the relay (1 = Keithley). | `bace/drivers/delib.py`, `bace/drivers/shutter.py`, `bace/drivers/routing.py` |

Parts the software does not address:

| Part | Role |
|---|---|
| Light source | A 520 nm laser diode, Nichia NDG7475, 1 W, in a copper module with a glass lens, coupled into the fibre. The code, the console and this manual call it the LED. |
| Light amplifier, ×4 (LOPV17) | Between the 33220A and the light source: the diode sees four times the 33220A level, through about 1 Ω at the amplifier's output. |
| Sample holder | Carries the amplifier board: the bias amplifier (×4, input V_i from the 81150A), the current output I_d to the digitiser, and the relay with its control cable from the DIO. The board contacts the pixel through the shortest pin, which keeps its rise time near 7 ns; only pixels B and E can be contacted. |
| Two ±15 V dual supplies | Power the light amplifier and the sample-holder amplifier. Each channel draws about 0.04 A when its amplifier is connected and working. |
| Photodiode at the sample, on CHAN4 | Shows the light pulse beside the current, for checking the delay. |

Sources for these parts, and for the limits below, are the group's training material in `Setups and Calibrations/` on the group drive: the BACE Quick Guide, the TDCF setup and measurement guides, and the BACE training video.

## Signal paths

| Path | Route |
|---|---|
| Light | 33220A → ×4 light amplifier → laser diode → 85 m optical fibre → shutter → sample, with a beam splitter to the 1918-C |
| Bias | 81150A Out 1 → holder cable V_i → ×4 bias amplifier (`pulse_amp`) → relay → device |
| Current | device → sample-holder amplifier → holder cable I_d → 50 Ω → DSO9054H CHAN2. The chain converts current to voltage at R_s = 5.192 V/A (`sense_resistor_ohm`); 2.3 |
| Trigger | 33220A Sync → 81150A External In; 81150A Trigger Out → DSO9054H CHAN3 |
| Timing check | Photodiode at the sample → DSO9054H CHAN4 |
| DC measurement | Keithley 2400 → relay → device |

The relay connects the device either to the amplifier and the digitiser, for a shot, or to the Keithley, for a DC measurement. The service refuses to move it while either source drives an output.

## Timing

Times are given from the 81150A Sync, which triggers the digitiser and is t = 0 of every record. The 33220A's inverted output polarity makes the rising edge of its own Sync the light-off edge; that edge arms the 81150A.

| Event | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 33220A drive edge (light off commanded) | −380 ns | `figures/bace-timing.html` |
| 81150A Sync, digitiser trigger | 0 | |
| Light off at the sample | +122 ns: 502 ns after the drive edge, of which 419 ns is the fibre and about 83 ns the LED driver | `rig.toml [timing]` |
| Field at the device | `delay_ns` + 47.1 ns; 137 ns at `delay_ns` = 90 | `trigger_offset_s` (2.2) |
| Integration window | from the field arrival + `t0_int_s` (−2 ns), for `t_int_width_s` (6 µs) | `run.toml` |
| Record | 1 µs before the trigger, 9 µs after | `timebase_ns_per_div` = 1000 |

At `delay_ns` = 90 the field therefore reaches the device about 15 ns after the light goes out. `delay_ns` sets this interval; a delay scan varies it.

## Limits

| Item | Limit | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 81150A External In | ±15 V | the instrument's front panel |
| DSO9054H input at 50 Ω | 5 V RMS | the instrument's front panel |
| Amplifier supplies | Connect or disconnect the amplifier leads only with the supplies off, and check the polarity of every lead: a wrong connection can destroy the amplifiers | the BACE Quick Guide |
| Relay control cable | Red to red on the sample-holder board; reversed, it destroys the relay | the training video |
| Laser diode current | At most 2.4 A, and the diode is meant for a constant-current driver. Here a voltage amplifier drives it, so its current is set by four times the 33220A level less the diode's forward voltage, over about 1 Ω, and rises steeply above the lasing threshold: a small increase of `level_v` can raise the current by amperes, most of all in DC mode, where the V_oc measurement holds it on | the seller's listing (item 181421784595) |
| Laser diode, typical | Threshold about 300 mA; forward voltage about 4.6 V at 1.6 A. Reseller figures for this diode class, not a Nichia datasheet | laserdiodesource.com, optlasersgrav.com |
| Laser safety | The source is a 1 W laser at the diode: wear laser goggles when the beam path is open, and align at a low level | the training video |
| V_coll | Increase \|V_coll\| only as far as the extraction needs (2.4); a large reverse bias such as −10 V can damage a device | the TDCF measurement guide |
| Digitiser input impedance | Set by hand on the front panel; the service does not set it. CHAN2 at 1 MΩ behind a 50 Ω feed-through, CHAN3 at 1 MΩ, CHAN4 (photodiode) at 50 Ω | the training video |

## Open points

| Point | Status |
|---|---|
| R_s | The training connects the I_d cable to CHAN2 through a 50 Ω feed-through, with CHAN2 at 1 MΩ, and calls 5.192 "the resistor of the amplifier". The value is used as R_s in every Q; how it follows from the amplifier's circuit is not documented. A capacitor calibration (2.4) tests it directly. |
| The diode's current at `level_v` | At the default `level_v` of 1.0 V the diode sees 4.0 V, near the lasing threshold, which matches `led_threshold_v` = 1.0 V. Measure the current across the amplifier's output resistor in DC mode at the levels in use, and set a ceiling on `level_v` below the level that reaches 2.4 A, with margin. Nothing in the software limits `level_v` from above. |
| The power meter's wavelength | `rig.toml [power_meter] wavelength_nm` is 530 nm; the diode emits at 520 nm. Setting 520 nm changes every intensity reading by the 1918-C's responsivity ratio between the two wavelengths, so compare one reading at both settings before changing it. |

## Temperature and light power

The cryostat setpoint, heater range, PID and ramp are set on the 331's front panel; the service only writes the setpoint and reads the temperature. A setpoint above `max_setpoint_k` (350 K) is refused. The 1918-C averages the 500 Hz light with its 5 Hz analog filter, so a reading is half the power of the source when on. The meter's wavelength is set to 530 nm in `rig.toml`; the diode emits at 520 nm.
